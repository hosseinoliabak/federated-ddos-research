Proposed topology and routing for the controlled federated DDoS study, v2.2 (PROPOSAL for owner review; not built or started). The whole lab sits inside one isolated colima VM, drawn as the outer rectangle and labelled “colima VM (isolation boundary)”: a root-namespace guard on the lab bridges, no route out of the VM, IPv6 off, and no NAT. Inside that boundary the figure separates the outside (RFC 5737 TEST-NET, dashed orange) from the inside (10.203.0.0/16, dashed blue). Outside, three upstream transit routers each form a separate autonomous system (TR-A AS 64513, TR-B AS 64514, TR-C AS 64515) and originate one TEST-NET /24. Each transit router serves a host-site box with three /26 host sites (A1–A3 in 192.0.2.0/24, B1–B3 in 198.51.100.0/24, C1–C3 in 203.0.113.0/24), and each /26 block holds about 40 bots, about 40 benign clients, and about 8 reflectors; the fourth /26 (.192/26) is reserved for infrastructure. An access link joins each host-site box to its transit router. The two ISP border routers (BR-1, BR-2; AS 64512) run ingress-pinned eBGP (TCP/179) with each transit router; all six sessions are drawn between the router circles, solid to BR-1 and dashed to BR-2, and the sessions table lists each full /31. BR-1 advertises only 10.203.10.0/24 and BR-2 only 10.203.20.0/24, so traffic for a victim enters at that victim’s own border router. Redistribution is absent in both directions, the inside reaches TEST-NET by a default route, and a pre-registered fallback of static routing everywhere is noted. Each border router is the metered Observation Point (IPFIX metering and the 1-in-4 HMAC sampler). Inside, OSPF area 0 runs across the border routers and provider edges, and each provider edge reaches its customer edge and victim customer (CUST-1 at 10.203.10.10; CUST-2 at 10.203.20.10), each running a listening TCP service, a UDP sink, and an HTTP server. Two paths are highlighted. The wide translucent solid band is one example data path, carrying attack and benign traffic together: host site, TR-A, BR-1, PE-1, CE-1, CUST-1. The wide translucent dashed band is the reflection path: a reflector replies to the victim from its own address, with no spoofing, here through TR-B, BR-1, PE-1, and CE-1 to CUST-1. The out-of-band management and services plane (10.204.0.0/24 and 10.204.10.0/24) holds the two points of presence (POP-1, POP-2), each a collector and federated-learning client that receives IPFIX exports from its border router, and the coordinator SVC-1, which runs FedAvg over the two clients and receives alerts on a separate process. Link addresses are shown on the figure; full interface names are in TOPOLOGY-IP-PROPOSAL.v2.1.md. The legend lists eBGP to BR-1, eBGP to BR-2, OSPF, data and access links, IPFIX export, model updates, and the two highlighted paths. Detection only; no filtering or mitigation. The figure is a proposed architecture, not evidence of a built or validated lab.
